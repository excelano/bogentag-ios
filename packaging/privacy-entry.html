Bogentag (iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch) — Privacy

Bogentag tells time by the Sun's position and, on the iPhone and iPad, lays out the calendars on your device the same way, so it works with two personal things: your location and your calendar events. This section says what it does with each. In brief: it reads both on the device, keeps nothing of your calendars, adds an event to them only when you set a moment, changes one only when you change it yourself, keeps the coordinates in use on the device, and sends nothing anywhere. Bogentag has no server, no account and no network code at all. Its App Store privacy label reads “Data Not Collected”.

Your calendars

With your permission, the iPhone and iPad app reads the events in the calendars on your device through Apple's calendar framework, EventKit, to draw them on the day you are looking at. It reads them only while it is on screen and does not copy them into storage of its own. It creates an event only when you set a moment, a zero-length event with an alert, in your default calendar for new events. It changes an event's start or end only when you set a new one in the event's details, and changes anything else about an event, or deletes it, only when you do so in Apple's own event editor. The calendars it reads are the ones your device already has, including any accounts you have added in Settings; Bogentag does not connect to those accounts itself. The Apple Watch app, the widgets and the complications do not read your calendars.

Your position

With your permission, Bogentag reads your location while the app is on screen, to find solar noon, sunrise and sunset where you are. It adopts a new position only when you have moved more than about ten kilometres from the one in use, since a smaller move changes the Sun's timing by less than the dial can show. Location is not used in the background. The iPhone app and the watch app each read their own position and do not exchange it. If you withdraw the permission, Bogentag forgets the coordinates it had.

What Bogentag saves

The app's preferences hold the coordinates in use, the calendars you have chosen to hide (by their identifiers, not their contents), your choice of spans or kiloseconds, the precision, whether clock times are shown, the zoom of the day view, whether the tour has been shown, and the timer and stopwatch: the amount and unit last set, when a running timer ends or what a paused one has left, and a stopwatch's start, total and laps. The coordinates in use and the choice of spans or kiloseconds are kept in storage each app shares only with its own widgets and complications, so that they can compute the reading without the app running. Nothing else is written. Removing the app from a device removes all of it from that device. The moments you set are not part of the app: they are ordinary events in your calendar, so they go wherever that calendar syncs and stay if you remove the app.

Notifications

With your permission, asked the first time you start a timer, Bogentag schedules one notification on the device for the moment the timer ends, and withdraws it when you pause or cancel the timer. On the iPhone a running timer also shows on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island. The device delivers and draws these itself; nothing is sent to a server for them. The alert on a moment you set belongs to the calendar event and is delivered by your calendar, not by Bogentag.

Nothing is sent

Bogentag contains no code that opens a network connection: no analytics, no crash reporter, no update check and no third-party libraries beyond Excelano's own open-source Bogenzeit engine, which runs on the device. Apple, separately, may show developers anonymous install counts and, from people who have turned on Share With App Developers, crash reports. That setting belongs to Apple and covers every app; it is on the iPhone under Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements.

Checking this

Bogentag's source is public at github.com/excelano/bogentag-ios. Calendars are read, moments added and event times saved in CalendarStore.swift, events are edited through Apple's editor in EventEditor.swift, location comes through SunLocation.swift, and the coordinates are written by AppGroup.swift. This text lives in the same repository, at packaging/privacy-entry.html, and its history there is the record of every change.
